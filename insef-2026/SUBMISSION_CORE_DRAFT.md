## Final working title

**THEORICA: Query-Efficient Reconstruction of Hidden Algebraic Systems from Translation Actions**

## Category

Senior — Computer Science & Engineering

## Research question

Can an initially unknown finite binary operation be reconstructed exactly from substantially fewer black-box queries than reading its full operation table, without being told in advance whether the operation is a group, loop, quandle, or another family?

## Hypothesis

For operations whose left translations form a faithful, sufficiently compact permutation action, discovering that action and a small permutation base should allow the complete hidden operation to be reconstructed from a sparse set of queries. Counterexamples encountered during validation should expose missing translations and permit the action model to expand rather than forcing an incorrect reconstruction.

## Contribution

THEORICA is a family-agnostic adaptive reconstruction procedure. It queries selected complete left translations, builds the permutation group generated by the observed translations, computes a small base for that action, and uses the images of base points as signatures for identifying unseen translations. A validation counterexample triggers acquisition of the falsifying translation, enlargement of the action group, and reconstruction retry. The method therefore combines structural compression with falsification-driven repair.

The competition claim is deliberately narrower than universal algebra learning: the method is evaluated under a scorer-defined compact-action promise and abstains rather than claiming success outside that promise.

## Experimental design

The public cross-family challenge is regenerated from public GAP catalogues. The learner is not given algebra-family labels. Holdout families use orders excluded from their corresponding development portions. The corpus contains 293 public algebras; the holdout contains 165 unique algebras evaluated under three frozen acquisition seeds, for 495 task/repeat cases.

Eligibility is computed by the scorer from the hidden full table only after evaluation. A case is eligible when learner-side left translations are permutations, the carrier-to-translation map is faithful, the full translation group has at most 20,000 elements, and the full action has a greedy permutation base of size at most 12. This yields 115 unique eligible holdout algebras, including 35 eligible non-group algebras, plus 50 unique out-of-promise controls.

The primary comparison is a same-representation random-row acquisition baseline. Both methods must reconstruct the same hidden table; query cost is compared only on paired-exact cases.

## Preserved negative result and repair

A frozen predecessor, Challenge v1.1, failed on 48 of 345 eligible task/repeat cases. All 48 failures were nonassociative loops. The failure mechanism was diagnostic: a partially generated translation group could agree on its current base while still omitting true translations. Fresh validation queries exposed these mismatches.

Challenge v1.2 changed one scientific behavior: when validation finds F(x,y) != h_x(y), THEORICA queries the complete falsifying translation L_x, enlarges the action group, and retries. The corpus, split, query budgets, eligibility scorer, random-row baseline, and success gates remained unchanged.

## Frozen public results

- Holdout task/repeat cases: 495
- Eligible cases: 345
- THEORICA exact reconstructions: 345/345
- Random-row exact reconstructions: 342/345
- Ineligible cases: 150
- THEORICA false acceptances: 0/150
- Median table fraction, THEORICA: 13.021%
- Median table fraction, random-row: 15.061%
- Mean calls on paired-exact cases: 174.20 vs 193.80
- Adaptive wins / ties / losses: 98 / 225 / 19
- One-sided paired Wilcoxon: p = 3.31e-14

On the larger SmallGrp holdout, THEORICA reconstructed 240/240 eligible cases, while the baseline reconstructed 237/240. Median table fraction was 10.981% versus 13.021%, with mean calls 230.16 versus 258.45 on paired-exact cases.

Cross-family correctness also held on 10 unique faithful connected holdout quandles (30/30 seeded cases) and 25 unique nonassociative order-6 loops (75/75 seeded cases). The 150 out-of-promise sampled-quandle cases produced zero false acceptances.

## Important limitation

The quandle and loop instances in the frozen public challenge are small. The 64-query validation budget usually exhausts the remaining table, so their total query fraction is effectively 100%. They establish cross-family exactness and safe abstention, not query efficiency for non-group structures. Efficiency evidence is strongest on the larger SmallGrp holdout.

A separately preregistered INSEF prospective challenge targets larger public non-group structures. Its outcomes must not appear here until its identifier manifest, hashes, eligibility/deduplication outputs, and frozen protocol are satisfied and the holdout is formally opened.

## Abstract — submission candidate

A hidden rule with n possible inputs in each position can require n² observations if its full operation table is read directly. This project asks whether internal symmetry can reduce that cost. I developed THEORICA, an adaptive algorithm that reconstructs finite binary operations by treating each left translation as a permutation, discovering the permutation action generated by observed translations, and identifying unseen translations through their behavior on a small action base. Validation is used as a falsification mechanism: a counterexample causes the missing translation to be queried and the structural model to expand. In a frozen public challenge regenerated from GAP catalogues, the holdout contained 165 unique algebras evaluated under three acquisition seeds. Of 345 cases satisfying a scorer-defined compact-action promise, THEORICA reconstructed 345/345 exactly, compared with 342/345 for a same-representation random-row baseline, while producing 0/150 false acceptances on out-of-promise controls. Across paired-exact cases, THEORICA used fewer queries with one-sided Wilcoxon p = 3.31×10⁻14. On the larger finite-group holdout it reconstructed 240/240 eligible cases using a median 10.981% of each table, versus 13.021% for the baseline. A frozen predecessor had failed on 48 nonassociative-loop cases; preserving those failures exposed a missing-translation mechanism and motivated the counterexample-driven repair. The results support a bounded claim: hidden operations with faithful compact translation actions can sometimes be reconstructed exactly from sparse structural observations, while the method can abstain when its assumptions fail.

## Judge-facing one-sentence explanation

Instead of asking every cell of an unknown multiplication-like table, THEORICA discovers the transformations hiding behind a few rows and uses their symmetry to infer the rest, then actively tests itself and repairs its model when a counterexample appears.

## High-priority judge Q&A

**Is this just memorizing known group tables?** No. The learner receives black-box operation queries and no family label. Holdout orders are separated from development within each family, and the challenge is regenerated from public catalogues.

**Why not simply query every row?** That is the n² baseline. THEORICA attempts to exploit repeated transformation structure so that unseen rows can be identified from their action on a small base.

**How do you know the inferred table is correct?** Exact reconstruction is scored against the hidden full table after the learner finishes. Validation queries are fresh learner-side checks; the scorer's full table is not available to the learner.

**Did the method ever fail?** Yes. Frozen v1.1 failed 48 eligible loop cases. Those failures are preserved. Their counterexamples revealed that the discovered action could be incomplete, motivating counterexample-driven action expansion in v1.2.

**Why is the random baseline fair?** It uses the same representation and reconstruction machinery but acquires translations randomly. The comparison therefore isolates the value of adaptive acquisition rather than comparing against a deliberately weak unrelated model.

**Does 345/345 prove it always works?** No. It establishes exactness on the frozen eligible challenge. The theoretical and empirical claim is conditional on the compact faithful translation-action structure.

**Why are the non-group query fractions not impressive?** Those structures are tiny and the frozen 64-query validation budget effectively exhausts them. They test cross-family correctness, not efficiency. A separately preregistered larger non-group challenge addresses this limitation prospectively.
