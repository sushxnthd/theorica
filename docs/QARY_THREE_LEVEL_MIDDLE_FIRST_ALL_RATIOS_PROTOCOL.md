# Frozen protocol: all-positive-ratio middle-first boundary

## Question

For the binary, four-copy, one-substitution, one-erasure problem conditioned on
a middle-level first query, does the six-face expression

\[
\max\{a+5b+c,\;2a+3b+4c,\;2a+4b+3c,\;3a+3b+3c,\;3a+4b+c,\;4a+2b+c\}
\]

give the exact optimum for every positive rational level-cost triple `(a,b,c)`?
At minimum, is it a universal adversarial impossibility boundary?

## Pre-registered proof route

1. Instrument every comparison in the exact 178,964-state projected adversary
   recursion with affine root/middle ratio coordinates.
2. Evaluate every positive-ratio boundary and open cell induced by the frozen
   comparison roots. Reject the proof if any cell contains an unaccounted root.
3. Require every projected source to lie in the six frozen exposure vectors and
   require their union to recover all six.
4. Open the ten frozen non-boundary rational ratios only after this protocol is
   committed. At each ratio, evaluate cheap-leaf, interior, and expensive-leaf
   exact planning instances against the six-face expression.
5. Reproduce the trace partition and prospective results with a standard-library
   audit importing no THEORICA modules. Require byte-identical evidence reruns.

## Interpretation rules

- A complete trace partition proves only the universal lower/impossibility
  boundary unless executable upper policies are also certified.
- Prospective exact matches establish tightness on the frozen cases, not by
  themselves a universal upper theorem.
- The first mismatch, extra source, or unaccounted crossing is preserved as a
  negative result; the protocol is not repaired after holdouts are opened.

## Scope exclusions

Unrestricted first actions, leaf-first conditioning, higher arity, other copy
multiplicities, deeper trees, larger fault budgets, and external novelty are out
of scope.
