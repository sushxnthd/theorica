# Fixed-scenario additive arrangement law

## Central claim

For a certified additive finite robust game with any fixed number `m` of
ambiguity scenarios, THEORICA can compute an exact minimax value, a nature
distribution, and an executable policy without enumerating the Cartesian
joint-action space for either value computation or policy recovery.

If `L` is the number of distinct component-local action-tie hyperplanes, an
exact dual minimizer occurs among at most

\[
\binom{L+m}{m-1}
\]

arrangement candidates. An optimal executable policy exists with support at
most `m`. Production implements the law with exact rational arithmetic for
`2 <= m <= 8`, with explicit fail-closed caps on boundary combinations,
marginal variables, and distinct cumulative-payoff states.

## Proof

For component action vector `u_{t,a}` and nature distribution
`q in Delta_m`, finite minimax duality and additivity give

\[
D(q)=\sum_t\max_a\langle q,u_{t,a}\rangle.
\]

Each summand is a convex piecewise-affine upper envelope. Its cells are cut
only by equalities between two actions from the same component. Across all
components these give `L` hyperplanes. Add the `m` facets of `Delta_m`. On
every cell of the common arrangement, `D` is affine, so a nonempty minimum face
contains a vertex. The simplex has dimension `m-1`; each arrangement vertex is
therefore generated by `m-1` independent boundaries chosen from the `L+m`
available boundaries. This proves the candidate bound.

At a minimizing `q`, complementary slackness restricts positive primal mass to
joint responses assembled from component actions tied at `q`. Additivity means
that every scenario payoff depends only on the component marginals. THEORICA
therefore solves the reduced marginal feasibility LP and couples the recovered
marginals by greedy common mass. A marginal basic feasible solution has at
most `T + m - 1` positive entries: the `T` simplex equations are independent,
while one active scenario equation is dependent because the `q`-weighted
payoff is constant on all tied actions. The coupling consequently needs at most
`m` joint responses.

HiGHS proposes a sparse marginal basis but is not trusted as a certificate.
All accepted probabilities are reconstructed from an exact rational linear
system, then independently replayed against every simplex and scenario
constraint. Pure-optimality classification is separate: an exact capped
dynamic program merges identical cumulative scenario-payoff vectors and never
enumerates joint tuples.

The exhaustive family contains three two-action components. Every action pair

The prospective holdouts are structurally frozen rather than selected after
scoring: 25 require five-way randomization by a scaled basis construction, and

values, and sparse policies matched the expanded exact solver.

## Scaling consequence

The 15-component witness has four actions per component and therefore

\[
4^{15}=1{,}073{,}741{,}824
\]

implicit joint actions. THEORICA used six distinct local tie hyperplanes,
generated 16 feasible arrangement candidates against the closed-form bound
330, and returned a valid exact policy. The value path never constructed the
Cartesian action set.

A second 15-component witness attacks the former recovery bottleneck directly.
Each component has five scenario-basis actions, all tied at uniform nature.
The retired recovery must reject the `5^15 = 30,517,578,125` tied joint
responses under its 500,000 cap. Marginal recovery used 75 variables, returned
a one-action exact policy with payoff 15 in all five scenarios, and classified
pure optimality through 3,876 distinct cumulative-payoff states. The
independent checker imports no planner code and replays the serialized policy
with `Fraction` arithmetic.

## Adversarial boundary and fail-closed behavior

The law requires certified additivity. Local component tables cannot reveal an
unreported cross-component interaction: two global games can share identical
local inputs while a bonus on one joint action changes the robust value.
Production therefore rejects missing additivity certificates, inconsistent
scenario dimensions, fewer than two or more than eight scenarios, excessive
boundary combinations, excessive marginal dimensions, exact reconstruction
failure, and excessive distinct payoff-state counts.

The first post-freeze implementation also produced a decisive internal
falsification: 2,640/32,768 classification mismatches. Values and policies were
valid, but the implementation incorrectly equated a returned mixed witness
with necessary randomization. The separate exact payoff-state classifier
removed all 2,640 mismatches; this failed gate remains preserved in the frozen
protocol and branch history.

## Novelty audit

Finite minimax duality, hyperplane arrangements, fixed-dimensional
piecewise-linear optimization, and LP sparse-support bounds are classical.
This result is not presented as a new general theorem in any of those areas.
The defensible THEORICA contribution is the explicit compositional law,
closed-form product-independent bound, exact proof-carrying implementation,
five-scenario falsification campaign, and executable integration that replaces
the former four-scenario ceiling.

## Limitations

- Complexity is polynomial in `L` only when `m` is fixed; the binomial bound
  grows rapidly with scenario count.
- Marginal recovery is polynomial-size in the tied local actions, but the
  exact pure-optimality classifier is pseudo-polynomial in the number of
  distinct cumulative payoff vectors and deliberately abstains above a cap.
- A floating solver proposes the marginal basis. Soundness is rational and
  exact, but completeness is conditional: an ill-conditioned basis proposal
  can cause conservative abstention even when an exact policy exists.
- Only finite, exactly rational, certified additive payoffs are covered.
- Cross-component interactions, approximate payoffs, sequential games, and
  continuous ambiguity sets are outside the result.
- The exhaustive sweep covers the complementary-binary three-component
  family; larger action shapes are tested prospectively rather than
  exhaustively.
